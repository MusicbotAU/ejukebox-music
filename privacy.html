The eJukebox Music app

Privacy policy.

No advertising, no tracking, and nothing sold to anybody. The app works fully without an account - and if you choose to sign in, this page says exactly what that stores and how to delete it. This page says what leaves your phone, and where it goes.

MusicBot Pty Ltd, trading as eJukebox. Last updated 13 September 2026.

In plain terms

What leaves your phone.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional and the app is complete without it. It exists for one reason: so your favourites follow you to another phone. You can sign in with Apple, Google or Microsoft - we never see or hold a password. From whichever one you choose we receive, and store, your name and your email address, together with the settings we sync: your favourites and the order you put them in, your recent stations, the stations you have hidden, your sort choice, your region and country, and your own star ratings. That is the whole list. It is kept in our own storage in Australia and is not shared with anyone or used to advertise to you. You can delete all of it from inside the app at any time - see deleting your account below.

The station list and the logos

The app downloads the station directory and the station artwork from our servers in Australia. Like any web request, our server sees your phone's internet address at the time. We do not keep a record of who downloaded what.

The radio itself

When you play a station, your phone connects to that broadcaster directly. We are not in the middle of the stream. The broadcaster sees the same thing it would see from its own app or a web browser: your internet address and how long you listened. Their privacy policy covers that, not ours. The song titles shown while a station plays come from the broadcaster's own now-playing service in the same way.

Star ratings

When you rate a station, the app sends the station, the number of stars and a random code that the app made up when it was first opened. That code is not your name, your phone number, your Apple ID or your device's serial number. It exists only so that rating a station again replaces your earlier rating instead of adding a second one. Ratings are stored in Australia, and only the averages are ever shown to anyone.

Near me

If you tap Near me and allow location, the app asks the phone for a rough position once, at the lowest accuracy iOS offers, and asks the phone which state or region that is. Only the answer, for example Victoria or Canterbury, is kept. Your position itself is never sent to us. If you are signed in, that region is one of the settings that syncs, so your new phone opens on the same stations. If you say no to the permission, the app works exactly the same and you pick the region yourself.

Diagnostics, only if you switch them on

Diagnostics are off unless you turn them on. If you do, the app sends player events (started, stopped, reconnected, which station) with the same random code, the iOS version and the app version to our monitoring service, so we can see why a stream dropped out. Nothing about you as a person is in it, and you can switch it off again at any time.

On the phone

What stays on your phone.

Favourites, the order you put them in, hidden stations, your sort choice and your own star ratings are stored on the phone. The app also keeps a copy of those settings in your own iCloud account, through Apple's key-value storage, so a new phone signed in to the same Apple ID gets them back. That copy is between you and Apple. We cannot read it. If you are also signed in to an eJukebox account, a copy of those same settings sits on our servers too, as set out above.

Recordings are files on your phone. They are never uploaded, and we never see them. They are for your own listening; the broadcast still belongs to the station.

Deleting the app deletes all of it, apart from the iCloud copy of your settings, which iOS removes when you turn off iCloud for the app or delete its data in Settings.

For the record

What we do not do.

  • No passwords - Apple, Google or Microsoft handle the sign-in and we never see one - and no phone numbers, ever.
  • No account required. Every part of the app works without one.
  • No advertising in the app, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking across other apps or websites, and no data sold, rented or shared with data brokers.
  • No analytics unless you turn diagnostics on yourself.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, microphone, camera or calendar.
  • Not directed at children, and nothing in it is designed to collect from them.

Your rights, and us

Questions, deletion, changes.

Deleting your account

If you signed in, you can delete your account and everything stored with it from inside the app, in a few seconds and without asking anyone: tap the person icon at the top right, then "Delete account" at the bottom of the sheet, and confirm. That removes your name, your email address, your favourites, your recents, your hidden stations and your ratings from our servers straight away, signs you out, and clears the copy on that phone. It cannot be undone, and there is no deactivate-only halfway house.

Deleting the app does not delete your account. If you have already removed the app, or you would rather not use it, ask us at the account deletion page and we will do it for you within 30 days.

Star ratings are stored against a random code the app made up, not against you or your account, so they are not part of your account and survive deleting it. If you want those removed too, the code is shown in the app's diagnostics sheet (press and hold the station title in the player); send it through the contact page and we will delete them. No questions asked.

We handle personal information under the Australian Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. If you think we have got something wrong, tell us first and we will fix it; if you are not satisfied you can take it to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

If the app starts collecting anything new, this page changes the same day, and the date at the top moves.
