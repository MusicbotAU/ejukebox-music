The 30-day trial

Thirty days. The whole thing in, and nothing to pay while you listen.

Not a demo link and not a login. The complete system goes into your venue, hardware and installation covered, and it runs for a month while you stand in the room and make up your mind.

Beat one

What turns up.

The player

A compact box, not a computer

Compact and solid-state. It holds the music on board, which is why a dropped line never reaches the room. We install it and set it up.

The channels

Built before we arrive

You tell us what your room does at each hour. A music director builds the run sheet around it, so what comes on at five on the first day is already meant for five o'clock in your venue.

The install

Two zones, wired and tested

Two zones are included, so the front bar and the beer garden can run different music from day one. We test both before we leave.

Beat two

What you do.

Nothing.

The channels change themselves at the hours we agreed. Nobody behind the bar goes looking for a phone, nobody forgets at three in the afternoon, and it runs the same on a Tuesday as it does on a Saturday. If you want it different, tell us and we change it at our end.

Beat three

The licence, stated straight.

Personal accounts like Spotify and Amazon Music are against their own terms of service to play in public, however good the playlist is. That is the whole argument, and it has nothing to do with price. eJukebox is licensed for business, so the music in your room is on the right side of the law from the first day of the trial, and nobody there has to think about it again.

MusicBot Pty Ltd - Australian owned, operated and human-curated.

Beat four

After thirty days - a conversation.

At the end of the month we ring you and ask what the room did. If it is worth keeping, we work out what it costs and you decide then, with a month of your own evidence behind you.

You will know inside a fortnight. Everybody does. It is the Friday you look up and realise nobody has gone home.
